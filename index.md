  <div>
    <p class="eyebrow">About me</p>
    <h2>Research interests and background</h2>

    <p>
      My research focuses on microwave remote sensing to investigate the
      physical properties of planetary surfaces and subsurfaces using both
      active and passive radar observations. I am particularly interested
      in understanding how electromagnetic waves interact with natural
      materials and how these interactions can be used to retrieve
      information about planetary environments.
    </p>

    <p>
      During my PhD, I investigated the surface and subsurface properties
      of Saturn's icy moon Enceladus through the joint analysis of active
      radar and passive radiometry observations acquired by the Cassini
      spacecraft. This work involved electromagnetic scattering and
      radiative-transfer modelling, heat-transfer modelling, and inversion
      techniques, including Bayesian approaches, to constrain the physical
      properties and thermal structure of the subsurface.
    </p>

    <p>
      I also have an engineering background, with a Bachelor's degree in
      Technology (B.Tech.) from the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT)
      Jammu, India.
    </p>

    <p class="eyebrow">Beyond my PhD research</p>
    <h2>Exploring radar techniques and applications</h2>
  </div>

  <div>
    <p>
      Beyond my doctoral research, I am interested in understanding the
      complete SAR imaging process, from raw radar measurements to the
      formation of focused images. I enjoy exploring radar data and methods
      for retrieving surface topography, including radar altimetry and
      radar clinometry, as well as using polarimetric information to
      investigate surface properties.
    </p>

    <p>
      My interests extend across both Earth and planetary applications
      of radar remote sensing. I also enjoy working on small scientific
      projects, developing codes, and preparing tutorials to explain
      remote-sensing concepts and techniques.
    </p>
