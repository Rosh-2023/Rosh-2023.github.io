My research interests lie in the use of microwave remote sensing and physical modelling to investigate planetary surfaces and shallow subsurfaces.

## Research interests

### Planetary microwave remote sensing
Interpretation of active radar backscatter and passive microwave emission to constrain the physical and dielectric properties of planetary materials.

### Electromagnetic scattering and radiative transfer
Modelling wave interactions with particulate and porous media, including scattering, absorption, penetration and emission in icy regoliths.

### Surface and subsurface characterisation
Combining remote-sensing observations with thermal and electromagnetic models to investigate layering, porosity, roughness and subsurface structure.

### Earth and planetary applications
Applying physical understanding and modelling approaches across planetary science and terrestrial remote sensing, including snow, ice and other natural surfaces.

## Doctoral research: Enceladus

My PhD research investigated anomalous regions of Enceladus through the joint analysis of active and passive observations acquired by the Cassini RADAR instrument. The work used electromagnetic and thermal modelling to explore constraints on regolith properties and subsurface thermal structure, with particular attention to the South Polar Terrain.
